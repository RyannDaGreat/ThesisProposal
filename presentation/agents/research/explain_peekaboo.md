# Peekaboo: explanation + slide spec

Machine-readable spec (layout + full speech): `presentation/agents/specs/peekaboo.json`. 7 slides (3 are one build), 646 spoken words. Revised in rounds 2 and 3 after examiner reviews.

## Plain explanation

Problem: segment "the thing the words describe" in a photo (e.g. "Harry Potter" in a photo with Hermione) without training any segmentation model. Peekaboo uses Stable Diffusion frozen, as downloaded.

Key idea: if you cut Harry out and paste him on any solid-color background, it is still a picture of Harry Potter. So the right mask is the one where every random-background composite still looks like "Harry Potter" to the diffusion model.

How it works, step by step:
1. A learnable mask alpha starts as noise.
2. Each step, blend the photo over a random solid color: composite = alpha * photo + (1 - alpha) * color.
3. Push the composite through score distillation (the DreamFusion loss, in latent space) with the prompt. The gradient goes only into alpha; Stable Diffusion never changes.
4. Alone, this keeps everything (the full photo is a fine "Harry Potter" picture). So add an alpha penalty (sum of alpha, coefficient 0.05): every kept pixel costs something. Pixels relevant to the prompt get strong gradients and stay; irrelevant ones get pulled to 0.
5. Pixel-wise masks come out chopped up, so alpha is parametrized through a bilateral filter guided by the photo's colors: similar colors get similar alpha, mask edges snap to image edges.
6. About 200 SGD steps per image.

Results: RefCOCO mIoU 0.204 (depth-bilateral) vs GroupViT 0.112 (trained 2 days on 16 V100s), LSeg 0.235 (trained for segmentation) still beats it. It "localizes the essence": "Emma Watson" grabs face and hair, not the dress. Making the RGB image learnable too generates transparent RGBA images from noise (first attempt at RGBA from an RGB-only model). Limits: hallucinates object shapes from background texture (worse with one-word prompts), sometimes adds extra parts, center bias from SD's training data.

## Likely committee questions
- **Why does the mask go to the object and not to all ones?** Without a penalty it stays near all ones (slide 3 shows this). The penalty makes every kept pixel cost something, so only pixels whose removal hurts the SDS score survive (slide 4).
- **Why random solid backgrounds instead of one fixed color?** With a fixed color the mask could use that color as part of the object. A new random color each step forces the masked-in pixels alone to carry the prompt (slides 2-3).
- **What gets gradients? Is SD fine-tuned?** Only the mask. SD v1.4 (text encoder, VAE, U-Net) is frozen. L = SDS + 0.05·Σα. Optimizer: SGD, lr 1e-5, 200 steps (slides 3-5).
- **SDS isn't a true gradient. What is minimized, and does it converge?** SDS drops the U-Net Jacobian, so it isn't the gradient of a fixed loss. In practice the mask settles in ~200 steps. The appendix shows implicit/bilateral parametrizations converge while raw pixels keep drifting (pixel-variance plot). There is no formal convergence guarantee. Say that plainly.
- **Numbers vs baselines?** RefCOCO mIoU: whole image .102, GroupViT .112, Peekaboo RGB bilateral .163, depth bilateral .204, LSeg .235. VOC-C: Clippy .539, GroupViT .578, LSeg .678; Peekaboo raster .340, RGB bilateral .520, depth bilateral .551. It beats GroupViT only on RefCOCO and never beats LSeg. It is coarse localization (slide 6).
- **Depth bilateral uses MiDaS. Isn't that supervised?** Yes, MiDaS is a trained depth network, so the depth variant leans on extra supervision. That is why the speech leads with the color-filter number (slide 6).
- **Cost?** No training of our own, but SD v1 pretraining was large, and each mask takes ~200 SD forward+backward passes per image and prompt. That is far slower than a feed-forward segmenter. No measured wall-clock number is in the thesis, so don't quote one (slide 6).
- **Why the bilateral filter?** A per-pixel mask gets fragmented. The color-guided blur inside the forward pass (raw matrix → bilateral blur → sigmoid; the filter is also run 40× with kernel 3 at init) ties alpha to color-similar regions and keeps edges aligned. VOC-C .340 → .520 (slide 5).
- **λ sensitivity?** The appendix ablates it: too big and the whole image is ignored, too small and background detail stays (thesis "Alpha Regularization Effect on Segmentation" figure) Not spoken in the talk; λ=0.05 is meaningless without the scale of Σα (up to 512² pixels) and of the SDS term.
- **Prompt sensitivity?** Real. The chapter says results are worse with one-word prompts, and the demo video used a longer prompt ("Harry Potter boy - daniel radcliffe - man with glasses"), which is why the line was cropped from the strip. Be ready to admit it.
- **Why not just read the cross-attention maps?** There is no attention baseline in the thesis. The honest answer: Peekaboo needs no access to model internals beyond the noise prediction, and it produces a mask that is optimized to be sufficient for the prompt. A direct comparison is missing.
- **Failure modes?** It hallucinates object shapes out of texture (bird/cat/dog), adds parts (a knife handle from bread), sometimes fails to converge (eyes), has a center bias from SD's training data, and inherits SD's biases (slide 6 names the hallucination).
- **"Essence": isn't that just a wrong segmentation?** It finds the region the prompt depends on most, which can be smaller than the annotated object. That costs IoU on RefCOCO (slide 6).
- **Relation to DreamFusion?** Same SDS loss, in latent space, applied to a 2D mask over random backgrounds instead of a NeRF. New: the compositing trick, the alpha penalty, and the bilateral parametrization (primer, slides 3-5).
- **Is RGBA generation evaluated?** No, it is qualitative only. "To our knowledge first" for an RGB-only model; LayerDiffuse (2024) later trained RGBA directly (slide 7).
- **Impact?** ~41 citations, CVPR 2023 O-DRUM workshop. Cited by "Your Diffusion Model is Secretly a Zero-Shot Classifier" (ICCV 2023). DGMO ported the idea to audio source separation.

- **Why not just read cross-attention maps (DAAM, Prompt-to-Prompt)?** We didn't compare to them, and that's a gap. Cross-attention maps are 64x64 or coarser and per-token, so combining them for a compound referring phrase ("bartender at center in gray shirt and blue jeans") is ad hoc. Peekaboo scores the whole prompt against a full-resolution composite. No DAAM/P2P baseline, and no center-prior or filter-only control either (slide 6, backup answer).
- **How was λ chosen? Is it tuned per dataset?** λ = 0.05, one value for every experiment. The appendix shows a segmentation figure at 0x / 1x / 2x and more: too large and the whole image gets dropped, too small and background leaks in. It is qualitative only, with no quantitative sweep and no held-out tuning shown. Σα scales with resolution, and all runs are at 512². I haven't confirmed whether λ was picked by looking at benchmark results. Ryan should check before claiming "not tuned" (slide 4).
